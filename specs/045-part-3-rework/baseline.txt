PHASE 1 — THE CONTROL, AND THE THREE TIMES IT CAUGHT ITS OWN TOOLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  snapshot.mjs   240 of 240 post-appendix paths byte-identical to relay-platform
  replay.mjs     644 states reproduced byte-exact through the merge machinery
                 242 paths, 0 conflicts, 0 drift, exit 0

THE CONTROL FOUND A BUG IN THE REPLAY TOOL ON ITS FIRST REAL RUN. snapshot.mjs
dumps the whole chain state after every chapter, so a path appears in every
chapter directory from its introduction onward. Reading "present" as "fenced by"
produced empty commits, and `git cherry-pick` refuses an empty commit — the
forced control came back with a conflict on nearly every path, at chapters as
early as 1.04. Fixed by counting a key only where its snapshot differs from the
previous key's. The state count fell 5,020 -> 644; the first figure was inflated
by unchanged repeats.

AND THE CONTROL ITSELF WAS WRONG TWICE BEFORE IT WAS RIGHT.

  1. `--order current` leaves every sequence untouched, so all 242 paths took a
     fast path and the control compared snapshots against copies of themselves.
     5,020 green states, zero lines of merge machinery exercised. `--force-replay`
     runs the merge regardless.

  2. The drift check then compared against replay's OWN INPUT. A corrupted
     snapshot was reproduced faithfully and the comparison passed. The target is
     now the platform file for the 193 paths the appendix does not amend — an
     independent one — and the pre-appendix snapshot for the 49 it does, counted
     separately rather than silently trusted.

FOUR PROBES WERE NEEDED TO MAKE THE CONTROL RED, AND THE FOUR ARE THE FINDING:

  perturb a mid-chain snapshot        green — the next delta cancels it
  perturb an appendix-amended path    green — its target IS the snapshot
  perturb the last-changing chapter   green — later directories hold the original
  perturb from that chapter onward    RED, exit 1, DRIFT infra.test.ts

So the control detects a wrong END state, not a wrong intermediate one. An
intermediate corruption that is later corrected is invisible to it. That is
defensible — the chain's contract is the end state plus every hunk applying — but
it is a limit, and it is recorded rather than assumed away.

THE PIVOT — REBUILD FORWARD, DO NOT RE-DERIVE
================================================================================

THE CONSTRAINT NONE OF THE FOUR MECHANISMS COULD HAVE MET, and it is written in
`relay-platform/README.md`:

    One git tag per chapter. Every tag is a runnable, tested state: check it out
    and the toolchain checks pass. Each chapter's SKIP AHEAD box names the tag to
    check out if you get stuck.

THE TAGS ARE PUBLISHED. Readers check them out. So the reorder was never a fence
problem with a tag problem attached — every chapter needs a working tree that
compiles and passes, and no snapshot re-derivation produces one. Re-hunking,
attribution-and-filter, three-way merge and running-maximum were all answering
the wrong question, and the 67% figure on `repository.ts` was the symptom rather
than the disease.

WHAT IS BEING DONE INSTEAD. A worktree at `part2-ch8`, branch `part3-rework`, and
Part 3 built forward one chapter at a time in the NEW order. The interleaving then
happens in the new order rather than having to be undone, and each chapter's
fences are the diff between consecutive tags — generated, not re-derived.

  new 1, 2   identical to old 1, 2 — the branch starts at `part3-ch2`, gates green
  new 3+     built forward, each ending in a commit + tag that typechecks, lints,
             builds and passes, with that chapter's `.mdx` regenerated in the same
             step

DECIDED: the rebuild need NOT land byte-identical on today's HEAD, only
equivalent. Insisting on byte-identity would reintroduce the interleaving
constraint through the back door.

TWO DEFECTS IN WHAT IS PUBLISHED TODAY, found by reading the tags:

  part3-ch8 and part3-ch9 are THE SAME COMMIT. Chapter 3.9's SKIP AHEAD sends the
  reader to 3.8's tree, missing that chapter's work entirely.

  part3-ch13, part3-ch14 and part3-ch15 DO NOT EXIST. Three SKIP AHEAD boxes name
  tags that were never created.

Four of Part 3's 24 chapters therefore have a wrong or absent tag. The rebuild
fixes all four as a side effect, and nothing in the old plan would have.

AND THE COMMIT LABELS DO NOT MATCH THE CHAPTER NUMBERS. The error registry was
built by `89fd038 feat(3.12): thirteen codes, one URL rule, and a typed thrower`,
while the chapter map calls that chapter old 14 — chapters were renumbered during
writing and the commit subjects kept the old numbers. Neither the tags nor the
commit subjects can be trusted as the chapter index; the published `.mdx` is the
only reliable source for what a chapter taught.

MOVING A CHAPTER CHANGES ITS CONTENT, NOT JUST ITS POSITION — measured on the
first divergence. Old 14 opens by counting the vocabulary and finding five codes
the platform had always sent and never registered. That narrative is a RETROFIT
and it exists only at position 14:

    codes registered at part3-ch2, which is new 3's base      5
    codes registered at old 3.14                             16
    codes at HEAD                                            27

At position 3 there is nothing to retrofit. New 3 becomes "establish the error
contract" — the registry, the typed thrower, the status-to-code filter and the
`docs_url` rule — over five codes, with every later chapter adding its own to a
registry that already exists. Better pedagogy, and NEW PROSE: FR-009's "prose is
preserved" cannot hold for a chapter whose subject was the mess it found.

WHAT THIS RETIRES. T021, T021a, T021b and the 29 conflicts go: there is nothing to
replay. `replay.mjs`, `runmax.mjs` and the snapshot machinery stay as the
MEASUREMENT that produced this decision, not as the mechanism. What survives
