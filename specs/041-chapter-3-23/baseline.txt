WHAT THE COMPOSITE KEY COSTS, WHICH THE SAD DOES NOT SAY

`PRIMARY KEY (message_id, edited_at)` means **two edits to one message at the same timestamp
collide** — a unique violation on the second insert rather than a second history row.
Postgres timestamps hold microseconds, so this needs two edits inside one microsecond on the
same message.

Not worked around, and the reason is not that it is unlikely. A surrogate `id` would accept
both rows and leave the history with two entries claiming the same instant, which is a
silent wrong answer; the key refuses instead. **A published constraint is a decision until an
ADR supersedes it** (Constitution VII), and this chapter has no evidence to supersede it
with — the largest fixture here is five channels.

`prior_text` IS NOT NULL, AND WHAT FOLLOWS (T022)

A deletion writes **no** edit-history row: a tombstone has no text to preserve, and the
column will not take a null. That is why FR-010 (3.23) refuses an edit on a tombstone rather
than defining what its history would say — the alternative is a row whose `prior_text` is the
text of a message the caller has already been told does not exist.

It also settles a question US2 would otherwise have to answer: **a deletion is not an edit**,
in the schema and not only in the wording. The two operations touch different tables.

THE BOUND, AND WHAT THREE OTHER SYSTEMS DO (T063)

    Slack       no event replay. `conversations.history` returns CURRENT state, and a client
                that was away re-reads. `message_changed` and `message_deleted` exist only
                as live events on the socket.
    Matrix      the other shape: an append-only timeline where a redaction is an event of
                its own, so a resuming client receives the redaction and applies it. Costs
                a timeline that grows with edits rather than with messages.
    IMAP        CONDSTORE/QRESYNC puts a `MODSEQ` beside the sequence, so a client asks
                "what changed since modseq N" — a second monotonic counter per mailbox,
                maintained on every mutation.

**This platform is already the first shape**, and FR-016b asks for that to be documented as a
property of a cursor rather than as a limitation. The cursor is a channel sequence; a sequence
orders CREATIONS. An edit does not create anything, so there is no sequence at which it
happened, and a cursor cannot address it. Adding one is Matrix's design or IMAP's, both of
which add a per-channel counter that every mutation has to maintain.

The user's decision, asked and answered during specification: **"Let's follow Slack."**

THE EDIT'S OUTBOX EVENT, AND THE ASYMMETRY WITH THE DELETION'S
