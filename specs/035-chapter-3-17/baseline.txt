  Reading the sections rather than the identifiers found a THIRD clause touching the
  sender, which no analysis pass had named:

    FR-MSG-08  "Deleting a message shall replace its content with a tombstone retaining
                sequence number, AUTHOR, timestamps, and deletion metadata."  P2, T

  It agrees with FR-MSG-15 and has never been built — chapter 3.15's T124 established that
  nothing writes a tombstone. Recorded, not amended: a clause that already says the right
  thing needs no edit, and the next chapter should not re-derive that.

T001/T002/T002a/T002c — the amendment, as `git diff` shows it

    + FR-USR-07   NEW      bot users with a description; shall not authenticate.   P2 T
    ~ FR-MSG-13   AMENDED  "any user" -> "a bot user of that tenant"               P2 T
    + FR-MSG-15   NEW      every message shall carry a sender                      P1 T
    ~ FR-RTL-05   AMENDED  "unique active users" -> "unique active persons"        P3 T
      FR-ANL-05   UNTOUCHED — meters "unique active users", still counting bots

  Two additive rows and two in-place edits. The two in-place edits are the whole reason
  this amendment is not additive-only: FR-MSG-13 said a key may send as ANY user and
  FR-RTL-05 capped ALL users, and leaving either as written would have left the document
  asserting both halves of a contradiction.

T002b — FR-MSG-13 is MET, not introduced

  The clause predates chapter 2.2's route. Chapter 3.3 satisfied it by sending
  unattributed, and messages.controller.ts has recorded that reading ever since:
  "A tenant's own server sending on a customer's behalf is FR-MSG-13, not a mistake."
  Thirteen analysis passes read this feature's claim that the SRS had no such requirement.
  A requirement satisfied by doing the opposite of what it says is harder to see than one
  nobody wrote.

T002d — FR-RTL-08's exception, stated in the amendment

  "Quota exhaustion shall degrade predictably: sends rejected with a specific error code."
  A bot's send is not rejected at exhaustion. In the blockquote, so it reads as a decision.

T002e — the other clause families, including the ones that gave nothing

    FR-AUT-09   dev-token mint, "in the development environment only". T040 refuses a bot
                there. NOT an in-place amendment: the clause grants an endpoint's existence
                and carries no universal quantifier, unlike FR-MSG-13's "any user" and
                FR-RTL-05's unqualified "unique active users". Three clauses looked alike
                and only two contradict.
    FR-MOD-03   every moderation action shall be audit-logged. NO AUDIT LOG EXISTS, and
                banning a person is already unlogged, so a bannable bot adds no violation
                to a P3 clause nothing has built.
    FR-MOD-04   the erasure endpoint is not deleteUser. SC-007 claims a deleted bot keeps
                its attribution; erasure removes everything. Do not let one be read as the
                other.
    EIR-API-03  status codes include 400 and 403 — this chapter's refusals are in the set.
    EIR-API-04  error shape: code, message, optional field, docs_url, request_id. FR-008's
                "with the field named" and T029c's docs_url anchor both conform.
    EIR-API-07  OpenAPI 3.1 published — P4, and no spec file exists in the repository.
    EIR-WHK-01..05  transport only. FR-012a's MessageCreatedData.user is governed by no
                external-interface clause.

  Seven families read. The empty answers are the point: the next chapter starts from seven
  rather than from one.

T004 — FR-USR-01 still holds

  "Relay shall not generate end-user identifiers." A bot's identifier is customer-supplied
  like any other. The rejected alternative is a synthetic sender the platform mints, which
  is what chapter 3.10 argued against and still argues against. A bot is DECLARED, not
  synthetic — the customer creates it, names it and describes it. That distinction is what
  T086d writes into 3.10's Trap.
