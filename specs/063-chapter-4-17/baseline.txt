T016  THE FIXTURE HAD TO BE RE-MEASURED, BECAUSE R2's RECIPE WAS NEVER WRITTEN DOWN
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

      `research.md` published **447,377 bytes** for the 800×600 image its whole walk was
      taken with, and recorded nothing about how those bytes were made — colour type,
      filter, entropy, seed. **A number without its method cannot be reproduced**, so the
      suite could not be built to hit it and every figure derived from it had to be taken
      again. Eleven figures across six artifacts moved.

      What ships, measured:

        800 × 600, colour type 0 (greyscale), filter 0, xorshift32 noise, seed 1
        deflateSync at the default level        480,756 bytes of IDAT
        the file                                480,813 bytes
        its thumbnail                           320 × 240, 30,612 bytes

      **AND THE FIRST GENERATOR WAS WRONG IN A WAY ONLY THE BYTE COUNT SHOWED.** The
      obvious `seed = (seed * 1103515245 + 12345) >>> 0` deflated to **23,284 bytes** — a
      62× ratio on data that was supposed to be incompressible. The product passes 2^53
      and JavaScript's floating point drops the low bits, so the sequence degenerates.
      Nothing about the image looks wrong; the only tell is the size, and it was only
      visible because the size was being measured for its own sake. xorshift32 instead.

      **GREYSCALE RATHER THAN RGB** is a third of the store, the quota and the PUT, and
      the file's existing 1×1 literal is greyscale too. **NOISE RATHER THAN A PICTURE**
      keeps the thumbnail comparison meaningful: a photograph of a white wall thumbnails
      LARGER than its parent, and `expect(thumb.length).toBeLessThan(image.length)` would
      then be a defect report about a correct platform.

T031  WHAT FR-MED-09's "RENDERS AS AN EXPLICIT REJECTION MARKER" CANNOT DISCHARGE
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

      **It cannot be discharged here at all, and the reason is structural rather than an
      omission.** *"Renders"* is a claim about a client, and **there is no client in this
      repository**: FR-MED-14's reference client is P4 and unbuilt. What this chapter can
      and does discharge is everything the clause depends on — the state is published, it
      survives in history, it is distinguishable from the two things a reader would
      otherwise confuse it with, and it is announced on a socket.

      So the clause is recorded as **met in its platform half and unmet in its client
      half, by structure**, which is 4.11's treatment of FR-MED-07 — *unmet by decision,
      vacuous until there is something to satisfy it*. The alternative is a chapter that
      claims a rendering nobody can see, and the honest version costs one sentence.

      runs back to back gave `p50 5,693 ms` because **a loop that waits for the thing it
      is timing synchronises with it.** Timed with `process.hrtime.bigint()`, never
      `date +%s%3N` — research R7 measured that appending nine digits of nanoseconds on
      this machine, which is how a harness once reported `slot 70934383ms`.

      All figures in milliseconds. min · p50 · max · mean, with n stated.

        n = 10          slot      9 ·   16 ·    20 ·   14
                        PUT       6 ·    7 ·     8 ·    7
                        verdict 1000 · 1994 ·  5756 · 2435
                        history   4 ·    5 ·     6 ·    5
                        link      4 ·    4 ·     7 ·    5
                        GET       2 ·    3 ·     3 ·    3
                        thumbnail 5 ·    7 ·     7 ·    6

        n = 25          slot      8 ·   11 ·    20 ·   12

      **EVERY STEP A CLIENT CONTROLS IS UNDER 20 ms AND THE ONE IT WAITS ON IS THREE
      ORDERS OF MAGNITUDE LARGER.** Slot, PUT, history, link, GET and thumbnail together
      are about 40 ms at p50 against a verdict wait of 3,398. **Publishing one total
      would describe a slow platform**; what is actually there is a fast platform that
      checks every five seconds.

T034  THE INTERVAL, SEPARATED FROM THE WORK — AND MEASURED FROM A DIFFERENT INSTRUMENT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

      The sweep's period is not the worker's own log. **It is the api's access log**, in
      which every pass announces itself as nine `GET /internal/media/pending` requests:

        twelve consecutive passes   5.37 · 5.39 · 5.40 · 5.40 · 5.40 · 5.41
                                    5.36 · 5.37 · 5.37 · 5.36 · 5.37      seconds

      **5,000 ms of interval and about 370 ms of work**, because the loop sleeps AFTER the
      pass rather than on a fixed schedule. 441 objects in the 24-hour window, 440 of
      which answer `HEAD` with a 404 — **the sweep's steady cost is a round trip per
      reservation nobody uploaded to**, which is 4.16's `outstandingReservations` finding
      arriving as latency instead of as a billing discrepancy.

      So the wait a client sees is **uniform on [0, 5,000] plus the work**, and the right
      thing to publish is that sentence with the interval beside it, not a single number.

T035  THE WORK, AS A RESIDUAL AND NOT A MEASUREMENT
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

      The smallest possible wait is zero — an upload that lands just before a pass begins.
      The smallest observed total is **433 ms** (n = 12). So

        HEAD + GET 480,813 B + scan + sniff + dimensions + thumbnail
          + rendition upload + verdict POST + the history read that sees it   ≤ 433 ms

      **That is a total minus a bound, not a stopwatch on the work**, and it is published
      as one. 4.13 measured a signed `HEAD` at 1.412 ms directly and ADR-31 has the scan
      curve; re-deriving either by subtraction would publish a worse number for a
      quantity that already has a good one.

      **AND THE SCAN IS NOT WHERE THE TIME IS.** Twenty INSTREAM scans of this exact
      fixture, straight to `clamd` on 3310: **min 2 · p50 2 · max 4 ms**, 20 of 20 `OK`.

T032b THE TAIL IS REAL, IT IS NOT THE TIMER AND IT IS NOT THE SCANNER
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

      4 of 25 trials took 19,294 · 30,153 · 32,269 · 36,164 ms, and 1 of 12 took 32,247 —
      five to six whole passes during which the object was pending, present in the
      bucket, inside the window, and not decided. Three candidates eliminated by
      measurement rather than by argument:

        the sweep stopped        NO   twelve consecutive passes, 5.36–5.41 s, no gap
        the scan was slow        NO   min 2 · p50 2 · max 4 ms over 20 scans
        the worker had died      NO   the api served it nine pending pages every 5.4 s

      **Left open and recorded as `gaps.md` 063-2** rather than diagnosed: this chapter
      adds no mechanism (FR-012), and a worker's scheduling is 4.13's. What is published
      is the distribution with its tail, because a p50 alone would say the path is
      reliably under four seconds and one trial in six is not.

      **AND THE TELL IS THE COLUMN BESIDE IT**: `link` reads 11–12 ms on exactly the
      tail trials and 4–6 ms on every other one. Whatever slows the worker slows the api
      at the same moment, which is the one real clue and points at something shared.

T032c AND I READ A LOG'S SILENCE AS A STALLED WORKER, FOR TEN MINUTES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

      `main.ts` logs a sweep **only when `ready > 0 || rejected > 0`** — deliberately, and
      the comment says why: *"a sweep over an empty backlog every five seconds would
      otherwise be the loudest thing in the log."* So an idle worker prints nothing.

      Measured, after four minutes of reading `docker compose logs --tail 1` and seeing
      the same line: 62 seconds of idle gave **0 sweep lines and 0 change in the ready
      count**, while the api's access log showed nine pending pages every 5.4 s
      throughout. **The worker was never stalled. Its log's gaps measure ARRIVALS, not
      LIVENESS**, and the two are indistinguishable from outside.

      This is 4.2's `/ping`, 4.9's unset credential, 4.10's bucket and 4.13's signature
      age at a fifth address, with the polarity flipped: not a check that cannot fail,
      but **a log that cannot say "I am alive and there was nothing to do."** Every
      conclusion drawn from those gaps — a 27-second stall, a 91-second stall, a hung
      process — was wrong, and the instrument that corrected all of them was **a
      different process's log of the first one's requests.**

T036  WHAT THIS LANE CANNOT SAY ABOUT THESE FIGURES
-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------

      One image, one tenant, one machine, loopback everywhere, and **one point on every
      curve that matters**:

        size        480,813 bytes. ADR-31 puts the scan at 52% of the work at 4 KiB and
                    97.7% at 100 MB, so the work term is a function of size and this
                    sample fixes it at one value.
        backlog     441 pending objects in the window. The pass cost is linear in that
                    number and CI's volume is EMPTY, so the same battery there would
                    measure a cheaper sweep — 056's finding, from the other side.
        contention  the worker, the api, Postgres, MinIO and ClamAV share one host with
                    the harness. The tail's correlation with the api's own latency is
                    exactly what that looks like.
        concurrency one object at a time. Nothing here says what the sweep costs when
                    fifty tenants upload in the same interval.

      **The number that travels is the SHAPE**: a wait uniform on [0, interval] plus
      work under half a second. The interval is a deployment property, and every figure
      above is a measurement of this deployment.
