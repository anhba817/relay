T009 — WHY A SEEDER HAS TO BE WRITTEN

    scripts/scale/seed.mjs creates users and channels for NFR-SCL-01 and writes NO
    message rows. The only comparable corpus this project has built —
    specs/034-chapter-3-15/baseline.txt:153 — says of itself:

        "The scratch database is not this lane and is not seeded by anything in the
         repository."

    So the instrument behind this project's one published million-row figure does not
    exist. That is why T011 creates one rather than reusing one.


T011 — THE SEEDER'S ARITHMETIC, EXECUTED

    scripts/scale/corpus.mjs — eight variables, readConfig, and a pure planFor.

    Its defaults reproduce the contract's example block, six of six:

        subject.messages_in_window      1,000,000
        subject.messages                1,333,334      ceil(1,000,000 x 120 / 90)
        created.messages                1,600,000      + 2 neighbours at 10%
        created.users                       6,000
        created.channels                    2,400
        created.channel_members            12,001      6,000 x 2, + the bot

    AND WRITING IT FOUND TWO CONSTRAINTS NINE ANALYSIS PASSES DID NOT. The documents
    said the neighbours and the extra thirty days exist "because a predicate that
    excludes nothing is not being tested either" — a rationale, in prose, that nothing
    enforced. CORPUS_ENVIRONMENTS=1 and CORPUS_DAYS=90 were both legal and both produce
    a corpus that measures one of the query's two predicates and publishes a number
    about both.

    The first draft of the guards used `>= 1` and `>= 90`, accepting exactly the two
    configurations the rationale forbids. Boundary probes caught it:

        CORPUS_DAYS=90         refused        CORPUS_ENVIRONMENTS=1   refused
        CORPUS_DAYS=91         accepted       CORPUS_ENVIRONMENTS=2   accepted
        CORPUS_MESSAGES=abc    refused        CORPUS_MESSAGES=-1      refused
        CORPUS_NULL_SENDER_RATIO=2  refused   defaults                accepted

    Both bounds are now strict and refuse rather than warn. `pnpm lint` passes; the
    first version failed it with two unused symbols written for T012's database work.
