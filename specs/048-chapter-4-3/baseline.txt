T023 / T024 — THE FIRST DRAIN, AND THE FOURTH NUMBER IS THE ONE THAT MATTERS

**The `ts` range is the rename passing.** A failed rename lands every row at the epoch, the
ninety-day TTL deletes them at insert, and all three internal counts agree at 0 — which is
why the published count is here as the anchor and not as decoration.

**And the 7 NULL statuses are the nullable columns earning their place on real data**: seven

status 0 for each and asserted that an endpoint replied with status zero.

T024 — A CORRECTION TO ANALYSIS PASS 2, FOUND BY RUNNING IT
-------------------------------------------------------------
    stream still holds 31 messages
    consumer analytics-ingester: num_pending 0 · ack_pending 0 · redelivered 0

Pass 2 found that an exhausted `max_deliver` leaves `num_pending 0` while the stream is
full, and concluded: **"the disagreement between them is the signal."** That is wrong, and
running the healthy case is what shows it.

`ANALYTICS` is `retention: Limits`, so **acknowledged messages stay in the stream until
`max_age` or `max_bytes`** — unlike a work-queue. So `num_pending 0` beside a full stream is
the NORMAL state, and it is also the stranded state. The two are indistinguishable from
those two numbers alone.

**The signal is the row count against the published count**, which is what T023 measures.
`num_redelivered` separates them as a secondary: a strand climbs it and a clean drain leaves
it at 0. T028 is corrected to record all three rather than the pair.

T028 — AND `num_redelivered` DOES NOT DISTINGUISH A STRAND EITHER
-------------------------------------------------------------------
Phase 3 corrected pass 2's claim that stream depth against `num_pending` is the signal:
under `retention: Limits` acknowledged messages stay in the stream, so a clean drain and a
strand look identical from those two.

**This phase corrects the fallback too.** The five records above were definitely
redelivered — delivered once during the outage, again after `ack_wait` expired — and
`num_redelivered` reads **0** afterwards. It is a **gauge of outstanding redeliveries, not a
cumulative counter**, so it reads zero once everything is acknowledged, exactly as it would
after a strand.

**The row count against the published count is the only signal that works**, which is what
T023 and T030's reconciliation measure. Two instruments were proposed and measured, and
both were wrong for the same reason: they describe the consumer's present state, and a
strand is a fact about the past.

T031 — WHAT `discard: old` DROPPED IS COMPUTABLE, NOT INVISIBLE
-----------------------------------------------------------------
The task expected the honest answer to be "the ingester cannot know". It can.

    first_seq  1        first_ts  2026-09-11T12:45:32.859Z
    last_seq   37       messages  37        bytes 17,618 of 1 GiB

**`first_seq` is the instrument.** `discard: old` drops from the front, so `first_seq`
advances past 1. A consumer knows the sequence it last acknowledged; if `first_seq` has
moved beyond it, the difference is exactly how many records were dropped while it was away.
Here `last_seq - first_seq + 1 == messages`, so nothing has been discarded.

T032 — SEVEN DAYS, AND NOW SOMETHING CONSUMES IT
--------------------------------------------------
The retention was chosen in chapter 3.20 while nothing read the stream: *"long enough for
an ingester to be down over a long weekend and short enough that the stream does not quietly
become the analytical database it is supposed to feed."*

**With `max_deliver: -1` the retention is now the ONLY bound on how long a record may
wait**, which raises the stake without changing the answer: seven days is still the window
in which a stopped ingester loses nothing, and T031 now gives a way to detect the case where
it does. At 17,618 bytes against 1 GiB, size is not the binding constraint here; age is.
