## Sabotage (T062, quickstart V3) — five mutations, all caught
M1  sign the re-serialised body, send the raw one   -> 1 failed  (invariants 4, 5)
M2  log the delivery-material response              -> 1 failed  (invariant 15)
M3  expand the event without claiming it            -> 2 failed  (invariant 8)
M4  drop `next_attempt_at <= now()` from the relay  -> 2 failed  (invariant 10)
M5  report the outcome before posting               -> 2 failed  (at-least-once)

Both mutated files verified byte-identical afterwards by md5sum.

TWO CORRECTIONS TO V3'S EXPECTATIONS, recorded because the table was wrong:

- M4 was expected to fail "invariants 9 and 10". It fails invariant 10 only.
  Invariant 9's tests are about claim EXCLUSIVITY — that a claimed delivery is not
  claimed twice — which does not depend on the dueness predicate. The table
  conflated two properties that happen to live in the same query.

- M1 would have PASSED before this run. The signature unit tests prove
  `signDelivery` is correct; nothing proved that `deliverOnce` calls it over the
  right bytes and puts the result on the wire, which is precisely where the
  re-serialisation trap lives. An integration test now verifies the signature
  against the raw body as received, using `node:crypto` and the documented recipe
  and nothing from the signing path. The mutation fails against it.
