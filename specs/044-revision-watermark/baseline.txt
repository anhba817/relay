**SC-005 — what a revision costs.** Two runs of 200 edits and 200 deletes each, over HTTP,
because that is the path a customer's revision takes.

              mean      p50      p95
    edit     10.28    9.46    19.47      run 1
             11.44   11.79    20.95      run 2
    delete    4.65    4.28     8.53      run 1
              3.83    3.66     6.84      run 2

    SC-005's band, ±10% of the two-run mean:  edit 9.77 — 11.94 ms · delete 3.82 — 4.67 ms

**An edit costs roughly twice a deletion**, and that is expected rather than surprising: the
edit path writes the message row AND an immutable history row in `message_edits`, where the
deletion writes the tombstone alone. The counter this feature adds is one UPDATE on both
paths, so it should move the delete's percentage more than the edit's.

**THE BAND IS WIDE BECAUSE THE RUN-TO-RUN SPREAD IS.** Edit means differ by 1.16 ms between
two runs of the same unchanged code — 11% of the smaller. **A 10% criterion against a
measurement whose own noise is 11% cannot distinguish a regression from a Tuesday**, and the
honest thing is to say so now rather than to discover it at close-out. T032 re-measures with
more runs, and if the noise still swamps the threshold the criterion moves to the p50 or the
run count goes up — recorded as a decision, not adjusted quietly.

**SC-005 — what a revision costs.** SIX runs of 200 edits and 200 deletes each, over HTTP.
T002 took two and said two was not enough; this is why.

                     before    after    delta    after stdev
    edit   mean      10.86    11.16   +2.75%    1.43 = 12.9% of the mean
           p50       10.62    10.83   +1.91%    1.88 = 17.3%
    delete mean       4.24     4.37   +2.99%    0.59 = 13.5%
           p50        3.97     4.12   +3.74%    0.85 = 20.6%

    Both MET. One extra UPDATE on a row the transaction already holds, costing under 3%.

**THE DECISION T002 ASKED FOR, AND IT IS NOT THE ONE T002 EXPECTED.**

T002 recorded that a 10% criterion against a measurement whose own noise is 11% cannot
distinguish a regression from a Tuesday, and proposed two remedies: move the criterion to
p50, or raise the run count.

**Moving to p50 would make it WORSE.** Measured over six runs, p50 is noisier than the
mean on both paths — 17.3% against 12.9% for the edit, 20.6% against 13.5% for the delete.
That is what a p50 of 200 samples does when the distribution has a long tail: the median
moves around inside a crowded middle while the mean is anchored by the whole sample. The
intuition that a median is the robust statistic is right about outliers and wrong about
this.

**What it would actually take**, at the variance now measured:

    edit mean   cv 12.9%   ~26 runs per side to resolve a 10% shift at 95%/80%
    edit p50    cv 17.3%   ~47 runs per side

Six runs per side resolves about 21%. So SC-005 as written **cannot see a 10% regression**,
and it did not see one here either — what it saw is +2.75% inside a band of +/-21%.

**CHOSEN: accept, with the measurement attached** — which is the first of T032's three
options and the only honest one, because nothing moved. The cost is one UPDATE inside a
transaction that already writes two rows, the direction is the one predicted at T002 (the
delete's percentage moves more than the edit's, 2.99% against 2.75%, because the delete
does less work to begin with), and every connection survived.

**The criterion is left at 10% and its resolution limit is recorded rather than the number
being quietly adjusted to whatever the lane now costs.** Anybody who wants SC-005 to bite
needs 26 runs a side, not a different statistic.
